\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}

Permanent magnetic materials with high energy density and thermal stability are essential for electrification, renewable energy generation, and precision industrial systems. However, computational discovery of novel magnetic compounds is hindered by the fundamental disconnect between chemical composition, crystal symmetry, and microstructural coercivity mechanisms. While intrinsic magnetic properties such as Curie temperature ($T_C$) and Néel temperature ($T_N$) depend primarily on local chemical bonding and exchange coupling, extrinsic properties such as coercivity ($H_c$) and maximum energy product ($(BH)_{\max}$) are strongly dictated by crystal anisotropy, grain morphology, and domain wall pinning.

\textbf{MagMat-Discovery} is an open machine learning and materials screening platform designed to address these multi-scale challenges. The system combines:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Curated Experimental \& Crystallographic Data}: 51,394 experimental records from the Non-Equilibrium Magnetic Materials Database (NEMAD), cross-referenced with DFT crystal symmetries (space groups 1--230, point groups, and theoretical densities) from the Materials Project funded by the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE).
    \item \textbf{Pretrained Foundation Models}: Equivariant atomic representations from MACE-MP-0 and 3D periodic crystal synthesis with thermodynamic convex hull distance ($E_{\text{hull}}$) from the DAO diffusion crystal engine.
    \item \textbf{In-House Domain Feature Engineering}: Multi-tier descriptors encoding Slater-Pauling valence filling, Goodenough-Kanamori superexchange proxies, spin-orbit scaling, and Stevens crystal-field operator terms.
    \item \textbf{In-House Graph Transformer}: \textbf{MagFormer}, a custom 384-dimensional self-attention graph transformer trained from scratch to embed chemical stoichiometry without requiring 3D coordinates.
    \item \textbf{Micromagnetic Physical Cascades}: Cascaded model coupling linking intrinsic anisotropy and magnetization predictions to extrinsic coercivity via the Kronmüller relationship, while strictly bounding energy products by the thermodynamic ceiling ($(BH)_{\max} \le \frac{1}{4}\mu_0 M_s^2$).
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Uncertainty Ranges}: Conformal prediction intervals constructed from out-of-fold residuals, guaranteeing distribution-free 90\% coverage for screening decisions.
\end{itemize}

Evaluated under leak-free 5-fold grouped cross-validation, the platform demonstrates strong predictive capability on ground-state magnetic phase (91.9\% accuracy) and transition temperatures ($R^2 = 0.82$ for $T_C$, $R^2 = 0.74$ for $T_N$). For extrinsic hysteresis properties, the models achieve moderate predictive accuracy ($R^2 = 0.46$--$0.62$), providing a fast, physically grounded ranking tool to prioritize candidate magnets before costly laboratory synthesis.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Magnetic Materials, Physics-Informed Machine Learning, Graph Transformers, Conformal Calibration, Coercivity, Permanent Magnets, Materials Screening.

\section{Project Background and Objectives}

Discovering high-performance permanent magnets with reduced rare-earth content is important for electric vehicles, wind turbines, and industrial machinery. The strongest commercial magnets rely heavily on critical rare-earth elements, primarily Neodymium (Nd), Dysprosium (Dy), and Samarium (Sm), which are vulnerable to supply disruptions, volatile pricing, and environmentally destructive extraction methods. Developing sustainable alternative magnets requires compounds that satisfy three strict physical requirements: elevated Curie temperature ($T_C > 400\,\text{K}$) to resist thermal demagnetization under operating conditions, high saturation magnetization ($\mu_0 M_s > 1.0\,\text{T}$) to deliver strong magnetic flux density, and substantial coercivity ($H_c > 800\,\text{kA/m}$) to withstand opposing demagnetizing fields.

Applying machine learning to accelerate this discovery faces three major scientific hurdles:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Disparate Physical Scales:} Target properties operate on fundamentally different length scales. Intrinsic properties like $T_C$ and $T_N$ correlate well with chemical composition because they are governed by atomic exchange coupling. Extrinsic properties like $H_c$ and $(BH)_{\max}$ depend critically on crystal anisotropy, grain morphology, boundary phases, and defect pinning, causing composition-only models to struggle.
    \item \textbf{Data Leakage across Polymorphs and Dopants:} Experimental materials databases frequently contain numerous entries of the same parent alloy with minor dopant variations or different measurement temperatures. Standard random data splitting places near-identical stoichiometries into both training and validation sets, producing artificially high validation scores that fail to generalize to novel chemical systems.
    \item \textbf{Violation of Thermodynamic and Physical Bounds:} Independent machine learning models predict each property in isolation, which frequently yields physically impossible combinations---such as predicted energy products exceeding the theoretical limit ($\frac{1}{4}\mu_0 M_s^2$) or finite coercivity predicted for non-magnetic compositions.
\end{enumerate}

MagMat-Discovery resolves these challenges by combining leak-free grouped cross-validation, multi-tier physics descriptors, a custom stoichiometric graph transformer (\textbf{MagFormer}), micromagnetic constraint cascades, and distribution-free conformal calibration into an integrated discovery engine.

\section{Data Curation and Split Design}

\subsection{Experimental and Crystallographic Data Sources}
\label{sec:data_sources}
The platform aggregates experimental measurements with crystallographic symmetry databases to establish a reliable foundation for model training and evaluation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{NEMAD Experimental Ground Truth:} We curate 51,394 experimental measurements from the Non-Equilibrium Magnetic Materials Database \cite{nemad2025}. These records supply experimental target values across 8 properties: ground-state magnetic phase, Curie temperature ($T_C$), Néel temperature ($T_N$), saturation magnetization ($M_s$), magnetocrystalline anisotropy ($K_1$), coercivity ($H_c$), maximum energy product ($(BH)_{\max}$), and paramagnetic Curie temperature ($\theta_p$).
    \item \textbf{Materials Project (U.S. Department of Energy / DOE):} From the open DFT repository developed under the U.S. DOE Office of Science \cite{materials_project}, we extract structural symmetries: space group numbers (1--230), Hermann-Mauguin symbols, crystal systems, point groups, and theoretical mass densities. To ensure models remain applicable to uncharacterized materials and to prevent target leakage, all DFT-calculated total energies, formation energies, and computed ground-state magnetic moments are strictly excluded from the input feature set.
    \item \textbf{Bilbao MAGNDATA:} Magnetic space group ground truths and Shubnikov symmetry classifications are incorporated for magnetic structure validation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Cleaning and Grouped Cross-Validation Partitioning}
\label{sec:data_splits}
Raw experimental records are sanitized by canonicalizing chemical formulas to coprime integer stoichiometries using \texttt{pymatgen}. Properties spanning multiple orders of magnitude ($H_c, K_1, (BH)_{\max}$) are transformed using base-10 logarithmic or symmetric-logarithmic scales to stabilize variance during training.

To ensure realistic performance estimates, cross-validation is performed using a 5-fold \texttt{GroupKFold} protocol partitioned strictly by \texttt{reduced\_formula}. Under this partitioning, identical chemical compositions and pure polymorphs (such as different crystal modifications of $\text{Fe}_3\text{Pt}$ or multiple measurements of $\text{Nd}_2\text{Fe}_{14}\text{B}$) are isolated entirely within a single fold. This guarantees that test folds contain only unseen compounds, evaluating whether the models can generalize across chemical families rather than memorizing known parent stoichiometries.

\subsection{Dual Representation Learning and Structure Synthesis}
\label{sec:physics_descriptors}
\label{sec:magformer_architecture}
\label{sec:dao_screening}

To capture both fundamental chemistry and crystal physics, the pipeline employs a dual representation approach:

\textbf{1. Multi-Tier Physics Descriptors:} Input compositions are featurized through four hierarchical physical tiers (detailed mathematically in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_physics}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \emph{Tier 1 (Stoichiometry and Elements):} Elemental mass fractions, atomic numbers, covalent radii, and Pauling electronegativities.
    \item \emph{Tier 2 (Electronic Structure and Superexchange):} Valence electron concentration (VEC) referenced to the Slater-Pauling maximum ($\text{VEC} \approx 8.3$), Goodenough-Kanamori superexchange proxies capturing anion-mediated exchange, and spin-orbit proxies ($\propto Z^4$) for heavy-element interactions.
    \item \emph{Tier 3 (Crystal Field and Anisotropy):} Crystal system indicators and second-order Stevens operator terms ($O_2^0, \alpha_J$) derived from Materials Project symmetries, indicating easy-axis ($K_1 > 0$) versus easy-plane preferences.
    \item \emph{Tier 4 (Equivariant Force-Field Embeddings):} High-order equivariant atomic representations extracted from MACE-MP-0 \cite{mace2022} for compounds with known crystal coordinates.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{2. MagFormer Stoichiometric Graph Transformer:} When screening uncharacterized or hypothetical alloys, 3D atomic coordinates are unavailable. To learn compositional representations directly from formula strings, we developed \textbf{MagFormer}, a custom stoichiometric graph transformer trained from scratch. MagFormer treats a chemical formula as a fully connected element graph where nodes are initialized with elemental properties and multi-head attention is biased by pairwise chemical differences ($|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j|$). Tri-part stoichiometry-weighted pooling yields a dense 384-dimensional representation vector that is passed to downstream tree models (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_magformer}).

\textbf{3. DAO Crystal Synthesis and Synthesizability Screening:} When 3D structural validation is required for a new stoichiometry, the platform invokes the \textbf{DAO} diffusion foundation engine \cite{dao2026}. The DAO-G generator synthesizes periodic crystal unit cells (lattice vectors and atomic coordinates) directly from chemical formulas. Symmetries are identified with \texttt{pymatgen}, while the DAO-P module evaluates the energy above the convex hull ($E_{\text{hull}}$). Compositions with $E_{\text{hull}} < 0.05\,\text{eV/atom}$ are flagged as readily synthesizable, ensuring hypothetical screening candidates are crystallographically viable.

\subsection{Specialist Ensembles, Physical Cascades, and Uncertainty Calibration}
\label{sec:ensembles_and_cascades}

For each of the 8 target properties, predictions are generated by an ensemble of five diverse regressors: CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost, ExtraTrees, and Random Forest. Predictions are synthesized via non-negative least squares (NNLS) meta-blending, which optimizes ensemble weights on out-of-fold predictions. A dedicated secondary boundary classifier resolves ambiguous phase candidates where predicted FM and AFM probabilities differ by less than 0.20.

To enforce physical laws across intrinsic and extrinsic targets, the pipeline executes a micromagnetic physical cascade:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Intrinsic properties ($K_1$ and $M_s$) are predicted first.
    \item Out-of-fold estimates of $K_1$ and $M_s$ are supplied as inputs to the coercivity ($H_c$) and energy product ($(BH)_{\max}$) regressors. This grounds coercivity predictions in the Kronmüller micromagnetic relation \cite{kronmuller2003}:
    \begin{equation}
        H_c \approx \alpha_{\text{micro}} \frac{2 K_1}{\mu_0 M_s} - N_{\text{eff}} M_s
        \label{eq:kronmuller}
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha_{\text{micro}}$ accounts for microstructural reduction (grain boundary defects) and $N_{\text{eff}}$ is the effective demagnetizing factor.
    \item Predicted energy products are capped at the thermodynamic limit: $(BH)_{\max} \le \frac{1}{4}\mu_0 M_s^2$.
\end{enumerate}

Every continuous prediction is accompanied by a distribution-free uncertainty range using split conformal prediction \cite{vovk2005}. The 90th percentile of absolute out-of-fold validation residuals establishes a calibration margin:
\begin{equation}
    q_{\text{conf}} = \text{Quantile}_{0.90} \left( \{ |y_i - \hat{y}_i| \}_{i=1}^n \right)
\end{equation}
The resulting prediction interval $[\hat{y} - q_{\text{conf}},\, \hat{y} + q_{\text{conf}}]$ provides rigorous 90\% coverage without requiring parametric error assumptions.

MagMat-Discovery demonstrates that integrating physics-informed descriptors, graph transformer representations, and micromagnetic cascades provides an effective, transparent platform for permanent magnet screening. \section{Physical Formulations for Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:appendix_physics}

This appendix provides the formal equations for the physics-guided descriptors described in Section~\ref{sec:physics_descriptors}.

\subsection{Goodenough-Kanamori Superexchange Transfer Factors}
In transition metal oxides, sulfides, and pnictides, indirect exchange between magnetic cations ($M$) mediated by non-magnetic anions ($X$) follows Goodenough-Kanamori rules. The transfer factor models the competition between ferromagnetic direct exchange and antiferromagnetic superexchange:
\begin{equation}
    J_{\text{ex}} \propto \cos^2\theta \cdot \exp\left( - \frac{|\chi_{\text{cat}} - \chi_{\text{anion}}|}{\chi_0} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is the $M\text{--}X\text{--}M$ bond angle, $\chi_{\text{cat}}$ and $\chi_{\text{anion}}$ denote Pauling electronegativities, and $\chi_0$ is a characteristic scaling parameter ($\chi_0 \approx 1.5$). When $\theta \approx 180^\circ$, strong antiferromagnetic superexchange dominates; near $90^\circ$, ferromagnetic direct exchange is favored.

\subsection{Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Interaction (DMI) Proxy}
In non-centrosymmetric space groups containing elements with strong spin-orbit coupling, the broken inversion symmetry introduces an antisymmetric exchange interaction (DMI) that induces chiral spin canting:
\begin{equation}
    D_{\text{proxy}} = \mathbb{I}(\text{non-centrosymmetric}) \sum_{i} x_i \left( \frac{Z_i}{100} \right)^4 \cdot \mathbb{I}(Z_i \ge 40)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function, $x_i$ is the stoichiometric fraction of element $i$, and $Z_i$ is its atomic number. The fourth-power dependence reflects the relativistic scaling of spin-orbit coupling ($\lambda_{\text{SOC}} \propto Z^4$).

\subsection{Crystal Field and Stevens Operator Hamiltonian}
In uniaxial crystallographic systems (tetragonal, hexagonal, and trigonal), the first-order magnetocrystalline anisotropy constant $K_1$ arises primarily from single-ion crystal field interactions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{CF}} = B_2^0 O_2^0 + B_4^0 O_4^0
\end{equation}
where $B_n^m$ are crystal-field parameters and $O_n^m$ are Stevens operator equivalents. The second-order operator is defined by:
\begin{equation}
    O_2^0 = 3 J_z^2 - J(J+1)
\end{equation}
where $J$ is the total angular momentum quantum number. The sign of the second-order Stevens factor $\alpha_J$ dictates whether the single-ion interaction favors an easy axis ($\alpha_J < 0$, uniaxial anisotropy $K_1 > 0$) or an easy plane ($\alpha_J > 0$).

\section{MagFormer Architecture and Pooling Details}
\label{sec:appendix_magformer}

This appendix provides the formal network equations for the MagFormer stoichiometric graph transformer described in Section~\ref{sec:magformer_architecture}.

\subsection{Node Initialization and Projection}
Each constituent element $i$ in a chemical formula is embedded through a combination of discrete atomic number embeddings and normalized elemental physical features:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_i^{(0)} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{node}} \left[ \mathbf{e}_{Z_i} \;\parallel\; x_i \;\parallel\; \mathbf{p}_i \right]
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_{Z_i} \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$ is a learned element embedding vector, $x_i \in (0, 1]$ is the stoichiometric fraction, and $\mathbf{p}_i$ is a normalized vector of elemental physical properties (electronegativity, covalent radius, valence, and Stoner parameter).

\subsection{Pair-Bias Self-Attention}
Constituent elements interact via multi-head self-attention modulated by a learned pairwise relational bias matrix $\mathbf{B}_{ij}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_{ij} = \text{softmax} \left( \frac{\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + \mathbf{B}_{ij} \right) \mathbf{V}_j
\end{equation}
where query ($\mathbf{Q}$), key ($\mathbf{K}$), and value ($\mathbf{V}$) projections are computed from $\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)}$. The pairwise bias $\mathbf{B}_{ij}$ is generated by passing elemental feature differences $|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j|$ through a two-layer multi-layer perceptron.

\subsection{Leakage-Free Composition Pooling}
To aggregate the multi-element representations into a fixed-length composition vector without leaking task-specific targets, MagFormer uses a composite pooling operation combining fraction-weighted sums, masked maximums, and global means:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{\text{comp}} = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^N x_i \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \;\parallel\; \max_{i=1 \dots N} \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \;\parallel\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{384}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the final transformer layer and $N$ is the number of constituent elements. The resulting 384-dimensional representation encodes complex non-linear chemical interactions and is supplied as an input to the tabular ensemble models.
