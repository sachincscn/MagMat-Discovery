\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}

Discovering permanent magnets with reduced rare-earth content is important for electric vehicles, wind turbines, and industrial machinery. Useful candidates must meet three criteria: Curie temperature $T_C > 400\,\text{K}$, saturation magnetization $\mu_0 M_s > 1.0\,\text{T}$, and coercivity $H_c > 800\,\text{kA/m}$. However, machine learning for magnetic materials is challenging because intrinsic properties depend on atomic chemistry while extrinsic properties depend on grain microstructure. Furthermore, random data splits cause leakage between identical compositions, and unconstrained models can make unphysical predictions.

\textbf{MagMat-Discovery} addresses these challenges through two primary objectives:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Leak-Free Multi-Property Modeling:} Simultaneously predict 8 intrinsic and extrinsic magnetic properties (phase, transition temperatures, and hysteresis behavior) using domain physics features, graph models, and 5-fold grouped cross-validation to prevent chemical formula leakage.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Constrained Screening and Uncertainty:} Connect intrinsic predictions to extrinsic properties via micromagnetic relations, quantify prediction confidence using 90\% conformal intervals, and provide an interactive web platform for candidate discovery.
\end{enumerate}

By combining experimental data, physical features, the \textbf{MagFormer} graph model, ensembles, and conformal calibration, the platform accurately predicts magnetic properties and provides physically bounded rankings for magnet discovery.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Magnetic Materials, Physics-Informed Machine Learning, Graph Transformers, Conformal Calibration, Coercivity, Permanent Magnets, Materials Screening.

\section{Data and Partitioning}

\subsection{Data Sources}
\label{sec:data_sources}
The platform uses three primary data sources:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{NEMAD Database:} 51,394 experimental records \cite{nemad2025} across all 8 target properties.
    \item \textbf{Materials Project:} Crystal symmetries (space groups 1--230, crystal systems, and densities) \cite{materials_project}. DFT energies and moments are excluded to prevent target leakage.
    \item \textbf{Bilbao MAGNDATA:} Magnetic space groups and Shubnikov symmetry classifications.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Cleaning and Grouped Splits}
\label{sec:data_splits}
Chemical formulas are simplified to lowest integer ratios with \texttt{pymatgen} (e.g., $\text{Fe}_6\text{Pt}_2 \to \text{Fe}_3\text{Pt}$). Properties spanning multiple orders of magnitude ($H_c, K_1, (BH)_{\max}$) are log-transformed.

To evaluate generalization to unseen materials, models are tested with 5-fold cross-validation grouped by chemical formula (\texttt{GroupKFold}). All entries with the same formula or crystal phase stay in the same split, ensuring test folds contain only new compounds.

\subsection{Input Representations and Crystal Generation}
\label{sec:physics_descriptors}
\label{sec:magformer_architecture}
\label{sec:dao_screening}

As outlined in Table~\ref{tab:provenance}, candidate materials are represented through domain physics features and graph neural embeddings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Physics Descriptors:} Formulations covering composition, electronic structure, and single-ion anisotropy factors (detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_physics}).
    \item \textbf{MagFormer Graph Embeddings:} Learned 384-dimensional representation vectors derived directly from chemical formulas via pair-biased self-attention (detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_magformer}).
    \item \textbf{Crystal Structure Synthesis:} When 3D atomic coordinates are required, structures are generated and evaluated for thermodynamic stability using the pretrained DAO framework \cite{dao2026}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ensembles, Cascades, and Uncertainty}
\label{sec:ensembles_and_cascades}

Target properties are predicted using 5-model ensembles combined through non-negative least squares weighting (Table~\ref{tab:provenance}), with ambiguous magnetic phases resolved by a specialized boundary classifier.

To ensure predictions obey physical laws, the pipeline enforces a micromagnetic cascade linking intrinsic parameters to extrinsic hysteresis behavior:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Intrinsic properties ($K_1$ and $M_s$) are predicted first.
    \item These feed into coercivity ($H_c$) modeling to follow the Kronmüller relationship \cite{kronmuller2003}:
    \begin{equation}
        H_c \approx \alpha_{\text{micro}} \frac{2 K_1}{\mu_0 M_s} - N_{\text{eff}} M_s
        \label{eq:kronmuller}
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha_{\text{micro}}$ represents microstructural defect pinning and $N_{\text{eff}}$ is the effective demagnetization factor.
    \item Predicted energy products are bounded by the theoretical maximum: $(BH)_{\max} \le \frac{1}{4}\mu_0 M_s^2$.
\end{enumerate}

Every continuous property is accompanied by a distribution-free uncertainty estimate calibrated on held-out validation residuals \cite{vovk2005}:
\begin{equation}
    q_{\text{conf}} = \text{Quantile}_{0.90} \left( \{ |y_i - \hat{y}_i| \}_{i=1}^n \right)
\end{equation}
providing guaranteed 90\% empirical coverage intervals $[\hat{y} - q_{\text{conf}},\, \hat{y} + q_{\text{conf}}]$ for candidate screening.

\section{Summary and Outlook}

MagMat-Discovery combines physical principles, graph neural networks, and micromagnetic rules to screen permanent magnets:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Accurate Screening:} Magnetic phase classification (91.9\% accuracy) and ordering temperatures ($R^2 = 0.82$ for $T_C$) serve as reliable initial filters.

\end{itemize}

Future work will incorporate grain-boundary phase models, domain wall pinning, and integration with automated synthesis platforms.

\section{Physical Feature Formulations}
\label{sec:appendix_physics}

This appendix provides concise mathematical formulations for the domain descriptors listed in Table~\ref{tab:provenance}.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Goodenough-Kanamori Superexchange:} Cation-anion-cation exchange coupling is parameterized as:
\begin{equation}
    J_{\text{ex}} \propto \cos^2\theta \cdot \exp\left( - \frac{|\chi_{\text{cat}} - \chi_{\text{anion}}|}{\chi_0} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is the bond angle, $\chi_{\text{cat}}$ and $\chi_{\text{anion}}$ are Pauling electronegativities, and $\chi_0 \approx 1.5$. Antiferromagnetic superexchange dominates near $180^\circ$, while ferromagnetic direct exchange is favored near $90^\circ$.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Interaction (DMI) Proxy:} Antisymmetric exchange in non-centrosymmetric crystals with heavy elements is modeled by:
\begin{equation}
    D_{\text{proxy}} = \mathbb{I}(\text{non-centrosymmetric}) \sum_{i} x_i \left( \frac{Z_i}{100} \right)^4 \cdot \mathbb{I}(Z_i \ge 40)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function, $x_i$ is atomic fraction, and $Z_i^4$ reflects relativistic spin-orbit coupling scaling ($\lambda_{\text{SOC}} \propto Z^4$).

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Crystal Field and Stevens Operator Equivalent:} Uniaxial anisotropy ($K_1$) arising from single-ion crystal fields is parameterized by:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{CF}} = B_2^0 O_2^0 + B_4^0 O_4^0, \qquad O_2^0 = 3 J_z^2 - J(J+1)
\end{equation}
where $B_n^m$ are crystal-field parameters and $J$ is total angular momentum. A negative Stevens factor ($\alpha_J < 0$) favors easy-axis anisotropy ($K_1 > 0$), while $\alpha_J > 0$ favors an easy plane.

\section{MagFormer Architecture Details}
\label{sec:appendix_magformer}

This appendix outlines the network equations for the MagFormer composition graph transformer.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Node Initialization:} Each element $i$ in a chemical formula is initialized via:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_i^{(0)} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{node}} \left[ \mathbf{e}_{Z_i} \;\parallel\; x_i \;\parallel\; \mathbf{p}_i \right]
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_{Z_i} \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$ is an element embedding, $x_i$ is the atomic fraction, and $\mathbf{p}_i$ contains normalized elemental features (electronegativity, covalent radius, valence, Stoner parameter).

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Pair-Bias Self-Attention:} Inter-element relations are modeled via multi-head self-attention with pairwise relational bias:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_{ij} = \text{softmax} \left( \frac{\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + \mathbf{B}_{ij} \right) \mathbf{V}_j
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{Q}_i, \mathbf{K}_j, \mathbf{V}_j$ are linear projections of $\mathbf{h}^{(l)}$, and the relational bias $\mathbf{B}_{ij} = \text{MLP}(|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j|)$ captures pairwise elemental differences.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Composition Pooling:} A fixed 384-dimensional representation is pooled across the $N$ constituent elements:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{\text{comp}} = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^N x_i \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \;\parallel\; \max_{i=1 \dots N} \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \;\parallel\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{384}
\end{equation}
combining fraction-weighted, max, and mean pooling without task-specific target leakage.
