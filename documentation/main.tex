\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}

Discovering high-performance permanent magnets with reduced rare-earth content is important for electric vehicles, wind turbines, and industrial machinery. Developing sustainable alternatives requires identifying materials that satisfy three physical criteria: elevated Curie temperature ($T_C > 400\,\text{K}$), substantial saturation magnetization ($\mu_0 M_s > 1.0\,\text{T}$), and high coercivity ($H_c > 800\,\text{kA/m}$). However, machine learning across magnetic materials is hindered by the physical disconnect between exchange-driven intrinsic properties and microstructure-sensitive extrinsic properties, data leakage across near-identical dopants and polymorphs, and the risk of unphysical predictions.

\textbf{MagMat-Discovery} addresses these challenges through four primary objectives:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Unified Multi-Target Modeling:} Simultaneously predict 8 intrinsic and extrinsic magnetic properties spanning ground-state phase, transition temperatures, and hysteresis behavior.
    \item \textbf{Leak-Free Grouped Evaluation:} Validate models using 5-fold cross-validation partitioned strictly by reduced chemical formula, ensuring identical compositions and polymorphs never cross train--test splits.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Enforced Cascades:} Connect intrinsic predictions ($K_1, M_s$) to extrinsic properties ($H_c, (BH)_{\max}$) using micromagnetic relationships and thermodynamic bounds.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Uncertainty and Screening:} Provide 90\% conformal prediction intervals for every continuous target and deploy an interactive web platform for candidate discovery.
\end{enumerate}

By combining curated experimental records, domain physics features, a custom stoichiometric graph transformer (\textbf{MagFormer}), specialist ensembles, and conformal calibration, the platform delivers strong predictive fidelity for magnetic phase and ordering temperatures, alongside physically bounded rankings for extrinsic hysteresis behavior.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Magnetic Materials, Physics-Informed Machine Learning, Graph Transformers, Conformal Calibration, Coercivity, Permanent Magnets, Materials Screening.

\section{Data and Partitioning}

\subsection{Data Sources}
\label{sec:data_sources}
The platform aggregates experimental measurements with crystallographic symmetry databases to establish a reliable foundation for model training and evaluation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{NEMAD Database:} 51,394 curated experimental records from the Non-Equilibrium Magnetic Materials Database \cite{nemad2025}, providing physical ground truths for magnetic phase, $T_C, T_N, M_s, K_1, H_c, (BH)_{\max}$, and $\theta_p$.
    \item \textbf{Materials Project (U.S. Department of Energy / DOE):} DFT crystallographic symmetries (space groups 1--230, point groups, crystal systems, and densities) from the open repository developed under the U.S. DOE Office of Science \cite{materials_project}. Computed ground-state energies and DFT magnetic moments are excluded to prevent target leakage when evaluating uncharacterized materials.
    \item \textbf{Bilbao MAGNDATA:} Magnetic space group ground truths and Shubnikov symmetry classifications for magnetic phase cross-referencing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Cleaning and Grouped Splits}
\label{sec:data_splits}
Raw records are sanitized by canonicalizing chemical formulas to coprime integer stoichiometries using \texttt{pymatgen}. Properties spanning multiple orders of magnitude ($H_c, K_1, (BH)_{\max}$) are log-transformed to stabilize variance during training.

To ensure realistic generalization estimates, cross-validation is performed using a 5-fold \texttt{GroupKFold} protocol partitioned strictly by \texttt{reduced\_formula}. Under this partitioning, identical chemical compositions and pure polymorphs (such as different crystal phases of $\text{Fe}_3\text{Pt}$ or repeated entries of $\text{Nd}_2\text{Fe}_{14}\text{B}$) are isolated entirely within a single fold. This guarantees that test folds contain only unseen compounds, evaluating whether models generalize across chemical families rather than memorizing known parent stoichiometries.

\subsection{Representations and Structure Synthesis}
\label{sec:physics_descriptors}
\label{sec:magformer_architecture}
\label{sec:dao_screening}

To capture both fundamental chemistry and crystal physics, the pipeline employs a dual representation approach:

\textbf{1. Multi-Tier Physics Descriptors:} Features are structured into four hierarchical physical tiers (formal equations in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_physics}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \emph{Tier 1 (Stoichiometry):} Elemental mass fractions, atomic numbers, covalent radii, and Pauling electronegativities.
    \item \emph{Tier 2 (Electronic Structure):} Valence electron concentration (VEC) relative to the Slater-Pauling maximum ($\text{VEC} \approx 8.3$), Goodenough-Kanamori superexchange proxies, and spin-orbit scaling ($\propto Z^4$).
    \item \emph{Tier 3 (Crystal Field):} Crystal system indicators and second-order Stevens operator terms ($O_2^0, \alpha_J$) derived from Materials Project symmetries.
    \item \emph{Tier 4 (Equivariant Embeddings):} Equivariant atomic representations extracted from MACE-MP-0 \cite{mace2022} when crystal coordinates are known.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{2. MagFormer Graph Transformer:} When screening uncharacterized alloys lacking crystal coordinates, \textbf{MagFormer} encodes chemical formulas directly into 384-dimensional representation vectors using relational self-attention over constituent elements (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_magformer}).

\textbf{3. DAO Structure Synthesis and Stability:} For candidates requiring 3D structural validation, the platform interfaces with the DAO diffusion foundation model \cite{dao2026}. DAO-G synthesizes 3D unit cells from formula strings, while DAO-P evaluates energy above the convex hull ($E_{\text{hull}}$). Compositions with $E_{\text{hull}} < 0.05\,\text{eV/atom}$ are classified as thermodynamically synthesizable.

\subsection{Ensembles, Cascades, and Uncertainty}
\label{sec:ensembles_and_cascades}

Each target is modeled using an ensemble of five diverse regressors: CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost, ExtraTrees, and Random Forest, combined via non-negative least squares (NNLS) meta-blending. A secondary classifier resolves close FM/AFM phase boundaries ($|P(\text{FM}) - P(\text{AFM})| < 0.20$).

To prevent unphysical predictions in extrinsic hysteresis targets, the pipeline executes a physical cascade:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Intrinsic properties ($K_1$ and $M_s$) are predicted first.
    \item Out-of-fold estimates are supplied to the coercivity ($H_c$) and energy product ($(BH)_{\max}$) models, grounding coercivity in the Kronmüller relationship \cite{kronmuller2003}:
    \begin{equation}
        H_c \approx \alpha_{\text{micro}} \frac{2 K_1}{\mu_0 M_s} - N_{\text{eff}} M_s
        \label{eq:kronmuller}
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha_{\text{micro}}$ accounts for microstructural defects and $N_{\text{eff}}$ is the effective demagnetizing factor.
    \item Predicted energy products are bounded by the thermodynamic limit: $(BH)_{\max} \le \frac{1}{4}\mu_0 M_s^2$.
\end{enumerate}

Every continuous prediction includes a distribution-free uncertainty range via split conformal prediction \cite{vovk2005}. The 90th percentile of absolute out-of-fold residuals defines a calibration margin:
\begin{equation}
    q_{\text{conf}} = \text{Quantile}_{0.90} \left( \{ |y_i - \hat{y}_i| \}_{i=1}^n \right)
\end{equation}
yielding calibrated prediction intervals $[\hat{y} - q_{\text{conf}},\, \hat{y} + q_{\text{conf}}]$ with guaranteed 90\% coverage.

\section{Summary and Outlook}

MagMat-Discovery demonstrates that integrating physics-informed descriptors, graph transformer representations, and micromagnetic cascades provides an effective, transparent platform for permanent magnet screening:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{High-Fidelity Screening:} Magnetic phase classification (91.9\% accuracy) and transition temperatures ($R^2 = 0.82$ for $T_C$) serve as reliable high-throughput filters.
    \item \textbf{Effective Ranking:} Extrinsic hysteresis targets ($H_c, K_1, (BH)_{\max}$) achieve moderate predictive correlation ($R^2 = 0.46$--$0.62$), providing a physically grounded pre-filter to rank candidate alloys before laboratory synthesis.
\end{itemize}

Future work will focus on integrating explicit grain-boundary phase representations, domain wall pinning energetics, and coupling the screening engine directly to robotic synthesis testbeds.

\section{Physical Feature Formulations}
\label{sec:appendix_physics}

This appendix provides the formal equations for the physics-guided descriptors described in Section~\ref{sec:physics_descriptors}.

\subsection{Goodenough-Kanamori Superexchange}
In transition metal oxides, sulfides, and pnictides, indirect exchange between magnetic cations ($M$) mediated by non-magnetic anions ($X$) follows Goodenough-Kanamori rules. The transfer factor models the competition between ferromagnetic direct exchange and antiferromagnetic superexchange:
\begin{equation}
    J_{\text{ex}} \propto \cos^2\theta \cdot \exp\left( - \frac{|\chi_{\text{cat}} - \chi_{\text{anion}}|}{\chi_0} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is the $M\text{--}X\text{--}M$ bond angle, $\chi_{\text{cat}}$ and $\chi_{\text{anion}}$ denote Pauling electronegativities, and $\chi_0$ is a characteristic scaling parameter ($\chi_0 \approx 1.5$). When $\theta \approx 180^\circ$, strong antiferromagnetic superexchange dominates; near $90^\circ$, ferromagnetic direct exchange is favored.

\subsection{Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Interaction (DMI) Proxy}
In non-centrosymmetric space groups containing elements with strong spin-orbit coupling, the broken inversion symmetry introduces an antisymmetric exchange interaction (DMI) that induces chiral spin canting:
\begin{equation}
    D_{\text{proxy}} = \mathbb{I}(\text{non-centrosymmetric}) \sum_{i} x_i \left( \frac{Z_i}{100} \right)^4 \cdot \mathbb{I}(Z_i \ge 40)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function, $x_i$ is the stoichiometric fraction of element $i$, and $Z_i$ is its atomic number. The fourth-power dependence reflects the relativistic scaling of spin-orbit coupling ($\lambda_{\text{SOC}} \propto Z^4$).

\subsection{Crystal Field and Stevens Operator Hamiltonian}
In uniaxial crystallographic systems (tetragonal, hexagonal, and trigonal), the first-order magnetocrystalline anisotropy constant $K_1$ arises primarily from single-ion crystal field interactions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{CF}} = B_2^0 O_2^0 + B_4^0 O_4^0
\end{equation}
where $B_n^m$ are crystal-field parameters and $O_n^m$ are Stevens operator equivalents. The second-order operator is defined by:
\begin{equation}
    O_2^0 = 3 J_z^2 - J(J+1)
\end{equation}
where $J$ is the total angular momentum quantum number. The sign of the second-order Stevens factor $\alpha_J$ dictates whether the single-ion interaction favors an easy axis ($\alpha_J < 0$, uniaxial anisotropy $K_1 > 0$) or an easy plane ($\alpha_J > 0$).

\section{MagFormer Architecture Details}
\label{sec:appendix_magformer}

This appendix provides the formal network equations for the MagFormer stoichiometric graph transformer described in Section~\ref{sec:magformer_architecture}.

\subsection{Node Initialization and Projection}
Each constituent element $i$ in a chemical formula is embedded through a combination of discrete atomic number embeddings and normalized elemental physical features:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_i^{(0)} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{node}} \left[ \mathbf{e}_{Z_i} \;\parallel\; x_i \;\parallel\; \mathbf{p}_i \right]
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_{Z_i} \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$ is a learned element embedding vector, $x_i \in (0, 1]$ is the stoichiometric fraction, and $\mathbf{p}_i$ is a normalized vector of elemental physical properties (electronegativity, covalent radius, valence, and Stoner parameter).

\subsection{Pair-Bias Self-Attention}
Constituent elements interact via multi-head self-attention modulated by a learned pairwise relational bias matrix $\mathbf{B}_{ij}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_{ij} = \text{softmax} \left( \frac{\mathbf{Q}_i \mathbf{K}_j^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + \mathbf{B}_{ij} \right) \mathbf{V}_j
\end{equation}
where query ($\mathbf{Q}$), key ($\mathbf{K}$), and value ($\mathbf{V}$) projections are computed from $\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)}$. The pairwise bias $\mathbf{B}_{ij}$ is generated by passing elemental feature differences $|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j|$ through a two-layer multi-layer perceptron.

\subsection{Leakage-Free Composition Pooling}
To aggregate the multi-element representations into a fixed-length composition vector without leaking task-specific targets, MagFormer uses a composite pooling operation combining fraction-weighted sums, masked maximums, and global means:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{\text{comp}} = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^N x_i \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \;\parallel\; \max_{i=1 \dots N} \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \;\parallel\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{h}_i^{(L)} \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{384}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the final transformer layer and $N$ is the number of constituent elements. The resulting 384-dimensional representation encodes complex non-linear chemical interactions and is supplied as an input to the tabular ensemble models.
